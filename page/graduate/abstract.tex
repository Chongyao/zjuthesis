\chapternonum{摘要}

物理仿真是计算机图形学、计算力学与计算机辅助工程交叉领域的核心研究方向，在工业设计验证、虚拟现实交互及具身智能系统建模等场景中具有广泛应用。此类方法通常需将连续物理场离散化为有限维代数系统进行求解；然而，当系统中同时存在局部高频"硬"成分与全局低频"软"响应时，往往会出现由软硬耦合引发的谱分离与条件数恶化，进而导致迭代求解收敛缓慢、时间步长受限及数值稳定性下降。传统代数预处理方法主要在矩阵层面修补病态性，难以充分利用问题背后的物理结构。针对这一挑战，本文提出以\textbf{基函数变换与表示空间重构}为核心的方法论，通过重新设计离散自由度及其伴生基空间，在表示层面隔离引发病态的局部高频刚度，同时保留决定宏观行为的全局低频响应。

围绕物理仿真中三类典型的软硬耦合来源，本文开展了系统研究。其一，针对\textbf{本构导致的软硬耦合}，研究不可伸长弹性细杆的动力学仿真问题：通过基于关节角的广义坐标变换将拉伸硬约束内建于运动学表述中，实现"零约束"的隐式动力学演化，并结合重构后 Hessian 的结构设计对称正定混合预条件子，实现了大时间步长与近线性求解复杂度。其二，针对\textbf{网格导致的软硬耦合}，研究包含畸变单元的有限元系统：提出算子感知的局部基函数优化与隔离框架，通过自适应细化暴露强连接区域、将病态局部聚合为多面体、并以 Dirac-wavelet 基函数重新张成解空间，成功将全局刚度矩阵的最大特征值降低约两个数量级，使共轭梯度法的收敛速度提升约50\%。其三，针对\textbf{算法导致的软硬耦合}，面向大规模模态综合法中的广义特征值问题：提出多重交错划分策略以及无 Schur 补的界面模态缩减方法，利用错位边界自然补足全局低频相位缺失，彻底打破分布式计算的通信壁垒，将低频特征值的求解精度提升三个数量级。

实验结果表明，本文所提出的基函数变换与表示空间重构方法能够有效改善三类病态系统的数值条件，显著提升仿真的计算效率、稳定性与特征谱提取精度，为面向软硬耦合问题的高效、健壮物理仿真提供了一条统一的表示空间重构路径。

\chapternonum{Abstract}

Physical simulation is a foundational research direction at the intersection of computer graphics, computational mechanics, and computer-aided engineering, with broad applications in industrial design validation, virtual environment interaction, robotics, and embodied intelligence. Such methods typically discretize continuous physical fields into finite-dimensional algebraic systems. However, when local high-frequency ``stiff'' components coexist with global low-frequency ``soft'' responses, the resulting systems often exhibit pronounced \textbf{stiff-soft coupling}, leading to severe spectral separation and ill-conditioning. This degrades iterative solver convergence, restricts stable time-step sizes, and compromises numerical stability. Traditional algebraic preconditioning mainly repairs the problem at the matrix level without fully exploiting the underlying physical structure. To address this limitation, this dissertation develops a unified methodology centered on \textbf{basis function transformation and representation-space reconstruction}. By redesigning discrete degrees of freedom and their associated basis spaces, the proposed framework isolates the local high-frequency stiffness responsible for ill-conditioning while preserving the global low-frequency responses that dominate macroscopic behavior.

This dissertation investigates three representative classes of stiff-soft coupling problems in physical simulation. First, for \textbf{constitutive stiff-soft coupling}, we study the dynamic simulation of inextensible elastic rods. A generalized coordinate transformation based on joint angles embeds stretch-related hard constraints directly into the kinematic representation, enabling constraint-free implicit dynamics. Combined with a symmetric positive-definite mixed preconditioner tailored to the reconstructed Hessian structure, the method achieves large time steps and near-linear computational complexity. Second, for \textbf{mesh-induced stiff-soft coupling}, we consider finite element systems with distorted elements. We propose an operator-aware framework for local basis optimization and isolation: exposing strong-connectivity regions through adaptive refinement, aggregating pathological local regions into polyhedral domains, and reconstructing the solution space using Dirac-wavelet basis functions. This successfully reduces the maximum eigenvalue of the global stiffness matrix by approximately two orders of magnitude, boosting the convergence speed of the Conjugate Gradient method by about 50\%. Third, for \textbf{algorithm-induced stiff-soft coupling}, we address large-scale generalized eigenvalue problems in component mode synthesis. We introduce a multi-level interlaced partitioning strategy and a Schur-complement-free interface modal reduction method that naturally compensates for missing global low-frequency phases using staggered boundaries, completely breaking communication barriers in distributed computing and improving the solution accuracy of low-frequency eigenvalues by three orders of magnitude.

Experimental results show that the proposed basis transformations and representation space reconstruction methods consistently improve the numerical conditioning of all three classes of systems, leading to substantial gains in computational efficiency, stability, and eigenspectrum approximation accuracy. Overall, this dissertation establishes a unified reconstruction-based route for building efficient and robust physical simulation pipelines for stiff-soft coupling problems.

\bigskip
\noindent \textbf{Keywords}: physical simulation; stiff-soft coupling; basis function transformation; inextensible rods; distorted mesh; component mode synthesis
